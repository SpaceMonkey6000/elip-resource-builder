Phase 4 · prove it

A resume claims. A portfolio proves.

For senior roles a resume alone rarely lands it. A portfolio of case studies (host on Notion, Google Slides or GitHub) shows your structural reasoning and trade-off judgment. Master the PRD and you signal you can guide engineering on day one.

The portfolio arc

Problem → Discovery → Solution & Prioritization → Execution → Results → Learnings. Anonymize confidential data, use realistic proxy metrics, and credit your cross-functional team.

Three pillars to show

Strategy (PRDs, Now/Next/Later roadmaps, OKRs) · Data (real SQL funnel queries, A/B test results) · Competitive (TAM/SAM/SOM, go-to-market).

📄

Lenny's PRD template ↗

The industry-standard simple PRD: Context, Usage Scenarios, Milestones. Crystallize the problem before any solutioning. Clone it into Notion for your portfolio.
✍️

Other great architectures to study: Linear (hierarchical, start with what won't change), Intercom's "Intermission" (fit it on one A4 page, plain English, no jargon). Write a mock PRD for a feature on an app you love.

The PM resume

Quantify five things. Translate everything.

The universal Resume Playbook covers ATS mechanics + the XYZ formula. Here's what's specific to PMs.

Metric categoryResume phrasing example
Revenue / cost"Increased MRR by 15% ($200k) over two quarters by launching tiered subscription models."
User growth & activation"Grew DAU from 10k to 25k in 3 months via an A/B-tested onboarding flow."
Conversion"Lifted trial-to-paid conversion 22% through multivariate copy testing on pricing."
Satisfaction / retention"Raised NPS from 38 to 62 (+63%) by launching an in-app feedback loop."
Time-to-market"Accelerated release cadence 30% by restructuring Jira epic dependencies."

🔄 Coming from another role? Translate it

  • "Managed client intake" → "Synthesized user feedback into product specs"
  • "Built a training program" → "Created scalable onboarding flows"
  • Speak PM, and recruiters read you as a PM.

🏛️ Structure & templates

  • Single-column, reverse-chronological, standard headings.
  • 4-line summary of core brand competencies (Stanford style).
  • Strong verbs: spearheaded, architected, orchestrated, streamlined.
